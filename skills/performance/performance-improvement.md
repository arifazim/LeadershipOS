# Skill: Performance Improvement

**Domain**: Performance Management
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: On-demand — invoked only after Stages 0–2 have failed to produce sustained improvement

---

## Purpose

Design and execute structured performance improvement plans (PIPs) that are specific, fair, time-bound, and genuinely oriented toward success — not manufactured documentation for a predetermined exit. A PIP is not a formality that precedes termination; it is the last structured attempt to create conditions for the person to succeed. If the EM already knows the outcome, the PIP is dishonest — and that dishonesty causes both legal and human harm.

**What the EM does automatically**: When entering the PIP stage, the EM:
1. Confirms the PIP is appropriate — that Stages 0–2 have been completed and documented, root cause is established, and the performance gap is genuinely within the person's control to close
2. Partners with HR from the start — not as a formality, but because the EM's role in a PIP is to develop and support, not to judge and decide alone
3. Designs success criteria that are specific enough to evaluate without subjective judgment
4. Provides the support and resources promised in the PIP — a PIP without EM investment is not a development plan; it is a notice
5. Evaluates outcomes honestly — if the person meets the criteria, the PIP closes positively; if not, the EM follows through on the stated consequence

**Ethical principle**: The person on a PIP is in one of the most stressful situations of their professional life. They deserve honesty about what is expected, genuine support in attempting it, and honest evaluation of the outcome. A manager who designs impossible criteria, withholds support, or delivers a predetermined negative outcome has abused the process. A manager who fails to follow through on stated consequences when criteria are not met has made the next conversation impossible. The PIP must be real on both sides.

## Inputs

### Required
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stage 0–2 documentation** | All prior feedback instances, written summaries, and commitments |
| **Specific performance gaps** | The observable behaviors that have not met level expectations despite intervention |
| **Root cause confirmation** | That the gap is within the person's control (not circumstance or capability requiring structural change) |

### Optional
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **HR partnership status** | Whether HR has been notified and is engaged in the process |
| **Legal/compliance context** | Any protected class considerations, leave status, or accommodation requirements |
| **Prior PIP history** | Whether this person has been on a PIP before at this company |

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Confirm PIP Is Appropriate

```
A PIP should only begin after confirming all six conditions:

✓ Condition 1: Stages 0–2 have been completed
  Evidence exists of multiple documented feedback conversations
  Written summaries were shared; commitments were made; commitments were not sustained
  
✓ Condition 2: The gap is within the person's control
  Not a capability gap that requires development (→ back to coaching)
  Not a circumstance gap (personal crisis, org condition) (→ accommodation)
  Not a fit gap that can be resolved by internal transfer (→ explore transfer first)
  The gap is behavioral or motivational — within their agency to address

✓ Condition 3: Expectations were explicit and documented
  The person knew what was expected; it was written; they signed or acknowledged it
  No one should be on a PIP for not meeting expectations they weren't told about

✓ Condition 4: HR has been engaged
  HR is not a bureaucratic formality — they protect both the person and the organization
  They catch legal issues before they become liability; they ensure fairness; they hold the EM accountable
  Do NOT begin a PIP without HR engagement

✓ Condition 5: The success criteria are achievable
  If the success criteria require sustained perfect performance that was never demonstrated before:
  the PIP is a performance theater, not a genuine improvement plan
  Criteria must be achievable; the person must have a real chance to succeed

✓ Condition 6: The EM will deliver the stated support
  A PIP that promises weekly coaching, specific resources, and clear feedback — and then doesn't deliver —
  is grounds for the termination being reversed or challenged
  Only commit to what you will actually do

If any condition is not met:
  Do NOT proceed with the PIP
  Return to the appropriate earlier stage or earlier skill
```

### Step 2 — Design the PIP Document

```
PIP structure:

Section 1 — Context (objective, not punitive framing)
  "This performance improvement plan documents the expectations for {{Name}} in the role of
  {{title}} at {{level}}, the specific gaps observed, the support that will be provided,
  and the criteria for successful completion. The goal of this plan is to support {{Name}}'s
  success in this role."

Section 2 — Documented performance gaps (specific and behavioral)
  Format: Dimension + Specific behavior + Specific instance + Impact
  Example:
    Dimension: Delivery and Scope
    Behavior: "Tasks committed to in sprint planning are not delivered by sprint end without proactive
               communication of blockers."
    Instances: "On {{date}}: {{item}} committed; not delivered; no blocker raised until review.
                On {{date}}: {{item}} committed; 4 days late; blocker surfaced on day 3."
    Impact: "Sprint velocity under-estimates have created planning failures in 3 of the last 4 sprints."
  
  Rule: Each documented gap must have a specific, dated instance.
  No impressions, interpretations, or general characterizations.

Section 3 — Success criteria (specific, measurable, time-bound)
  Format: Behavior + Measurement + Timeline + Evaluation method
  Example:
    "For the 60-day duration of this plan, {{Name}} will deliver all sprint-committed items
    by the sprint end, or communicate blockers within 24 hours of identifying them,
    with no more than one exception per sprint."
    
    Evaluation: "This will be evaluated by reviewing sprint completion data and blocker communication
    logs at the 30-day and 60-day check-ins."
  
  Rule: Success criteria must be specific enough that two people independently reviewing
  the evidence would reach the same conclusion about whether criteria were met.
  Subjective criteria ("demonstrates improved communication") are not evaluable.

Section 4 — Support provided
  Be specific about what the EM will do:
  ✓ "Weekly 30-minute coaching session with the EM every Monday at 10am"
  ✓ "Access to {{training resource}} to address the {{specific skill}} gap"
  ✓ "Introduction to {{senior engineer}} for architectural guidance on the current project"
  ✓ "Written feedback within 24 hours of each sprint retrospective"
  ✗ "Manager support as needed" — this is meaningless and will not be provided

Section 5 — Timeline and check-ins
  PIP duration: Typically 30–90 days depending on the gap severity and role complexity
    30 days: Clear behavioral change expected; relatively simple gap
    60 days: Pattern of behavior change; moderate complexity
    90 days: Complex capability or scope gap; extended demonstration period
  
  Formal check-ins: Every 2 weeks; documented; shared with HR
  
  Check-in format:
    "Since last check-in, here is what was expected: {{criteria}}"
    "Here is what was observed: {{specific behavior + evidence}}"
    "Assessment: On track / At risk / Not meeting criteria"
    "Next steps: {{specific}}"

Section 6 — Consequences
  State clearly and specifically what happens at each outcome:
  "If {{Name}} meets all success criteria by {{date}}: the PIP closes successfully.
  {{Name}} will return to standard performance management. No further consequences.
  If {{Name}} does not meet the success criteria: {{Name}}'s employment will be reviewed
  for separation, in accordance with company policy."
  
  Rule: Never imply or hint at termination without stating it explicitly in the consequences section.
  Never state termination is the consequence without being prepared to follow through.
```

### Step 3 — Deliver the PIP Conversation

```
The PIP conversation is one of the most difficult management conversations. Prepare specifically.

Before the conversation:
  Review the document with HR before delivering; HR should be present or available
  Know the answers to: "Can I appeal this?" | "What happens if I resign?" |
  "Will this affect my severance?" | "How will this be noted in my employment record?"

Opening the conversation:
  Direct, not apologetic: "I want to talk to you about a formal step we're taking in your
  performance management. This is a Performance Improvement Plan."
  
  State the purpose honestly: "The goal of this plan is to give you a structured path to
  succeed in this role. I want you to succeed. This is the last formal step before we would
  have to consider a different outcome."

Delivering the content:
  Walk through each section; do not rush; give them time to read
  "I want to make sure you understand the specific expectations. Can you tell me in your own words
  what you're hearing as the criteria?"
  This is not a quiz — it ensures shared understanding before they sign

Responding to reactions:

  Reaction — Shock/denial: "I had no idea this was coming"
    If true: The EM failed to give adequate prior feedback; acknowledge it
    "I should have been more direct earlier. I own that. And the performance gap still needs to be addressed."
    If not true: "We've had this conversation {{N}} times. Here is the documentation."

  Reaction — Anger: "This is unfair"
    Stay calm: "I hear that you feel that way. I want to give you the chance to respond.
    Tell me specifically what you believe is inaccurate in this document."
    If they identify a genuine factual error: correct it
    If they don't: "I understand you disagree. The plan stands. Here's what I'd like to ask of you: give it a real try."

  Reaction — Acceptance and urgency: "What do I need to do?"
    This is the best response; stay focused
    Walk through the support commitments; make them specific
    "Starting this week, here's what we're going to do differently together..."

After the conversation:
  Send the signed PIP document to HR
  Calendar the 2-week check-ins immediately
  Begin the support commitments on day 1, not "when we get to it"
```

### Step 4 — Evaluate and Close the PIP

```
At every check-in:
  Review evidence against criteria — not impressions against criteria
  Write the check-in assessment immediately; share with HR and the person
  
  On-track: "Through the first 30 days, {{Name}} has met {{N}} of {{N}} criteria.
  Specifically: {{evidence}}. The plan continues."
  
  At risk: "Through the first 30 days, {{Name}} has met {{N}} of {{N}} criteria.
  The criterion at risk is {{specific}}. Here is what I observed: {{evidence}}.
  Here is what needs to change in the next 30 days for the plan to close successfully: {{specific}}."
  
  Not meeting criteria: "Through the first 30 days, {{Name}} has not met the following criteria:
  {{specific criteria with evidence}}. Per the PIP document, this triggers {{consequence}}.
  I'm sharing this assessment with HR."

At PIP close (positive):
  Formal document: "{{Name}} has met all success criteria for the 60-day performance improvement plan.
  The plan closes successfully. Standard performance management resumes."
  1:1 conversation: "I want to acknowledge what you did. Meeting these criteria while under this
  pressure was genuinely hard. Here's what I observed: {{specific}}."
  The relationship after a successful PIP: Do not treat the person as permanently suspect.
  A successful PIP means they rose to the challenge. Invest in their success going forward.

At PIP close (negative):
  The consequence stated in the PIP must be followed — this is an integrity requirement
  Loop in HR immediately; do not delay; do not soften the outcome through delay
  Separation conversation: separate from the PIP close; specific; supported by HR
  
  What the EM says:
  "I have to tell you that you have not met the criteria in the performance improvement plan.
  Specifically: {{criteria}} was not met. {{Evidence}}.
  As stated in the plan, we are moving forward with a separation. HR will walk you through the process."
```

### Step 5 — After the PIP — Team and EM Recovery

```
After a PIP closes (either direction):

Team communication:
  If separation: "{{Name}} is no longer with the team. I want to acknowledge the transition
  and give us time to figure out what this means for our work."
  Do not: Explain the PIP or performance issues to the team
  Do: Redistribute work; acknowledge the load; protect team morale

If the PIP succeeds:
  Watch for regression in the 60–90 days after the PIP closes; it is common
  The first sign of regression: have the conversation immediately
  "I want to name something I'm seeing. Given what we went through together, I want to make
  sure we address this quickly."

EM self-reflection after any PIP:
  "Could Stage 0 or Stage 1 feedback have prevented this?"
  "Did I wait too long to have the initial difficult conversation?"
  "Did I give the person a real chance to succeed, or was this predetermined?"
  The answers will improve the next feedback loop.
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the PIP readiness assessment reveal?

├── CONDITIONS NOT ALL MET — DO NOT PROCEED WITH PIP
│   └── Identify which condition is missing:
│       Missing Stage 0–2: Give the feedback first; document it; give 30 days
│       Capability gap: → Coaching + growth plan; not PIP
│       Circumstance gap: → Accommodation; not PIP
│       Fit gap: → Internal transfer exploration; not PIP
│       Criteria not achievable: Redesign; a PIP with impossible criteria is dishonest

├── ALL CONDITIONS MET — PROCEED
│   └── Engage HR before drafting the document
│       Draft with HR review; schedule the conversation
│       Have the conversation; deliver the support commitments on day 1
│       Calendar all check-ins before leaving the conversation

├── PIP ONGOING — AT-RISK CHECK-IN
│   └── Name the risk specifically at the check-in
│       "Here's what criteria is at risk and what I'm observing."
│       Increase support — do not reduce it when the person is struggling
│       Document the check-in; share with HR immediately

├── PIP CRITERIA MET AT CLOSE
│   └── Close positively with specificity
│       Do not treat the person as permanently suspect
│       Monitor for 90 days; have the conversation immediately if regression appears

├── PIP CRITERIA NOT MET AT CLOSE
│   └── Follow through on stated consequence — this is an integrity requirement
│       Engage HR immediately; do not delay
│       Separation process follows HR protocol

└── PERSON RESIGNS DURING PIP
    └── Process the resignation normally; do not indicate they "got ahead of it"
        Conduct an honest exit conversation if they're willing
        Learn: What failed earlier in the feedback loop that produced this outcome?
```

## Confidence Score

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| All six conditions confirmed before PIP | Base: 65% |
| HR engaged before drafting | +15% |
| Success criteria are specific and evaluable | +10% |
| EM support commitments are specific and delivered | +10% |
| Check-ins documented and shared with HR | +5% |
| PIP used for capability gap (wrong tool) | −40% (disqualifying) |
| PIP with predetermined outcome (dishonest) | −50% (disqualifying) |
| Success criteria are subjective or vague | −25% |

---

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Predetermined PIP** | EM designs criteria the person cannot meet; outcome is decided before the plan starts | If the outcome is predetermined, do not use a PIP. Have the honest conversation about separation. |
| **PIP without prior feedback** | Person is surprised; no Stage 0–2 documentation exists | Return to Stage 0. You cannot PIP someone for expectations they weren't told about. |
| **Support not delivered** | PIP promises weekly coaching; EM cancels 4 of 6 sessions | If you commit to support, deliver it. Failure to provide support is grounds for the process to be challenged. |
| **Subjective criteria** | "Must demonstrate improved communication" — no one can evaluate this objectively | Every criterion must have a specific, observable, measurable component. If you can't count or see it, rewrite it. |
| **No follow-through** | Criteria not met; EM doesn't follow through on stated consequence | If you stated a consequence and then don't deliver it, the next difficult conversation becomes impossible. |
