# Skill: Executive Feedback

**Domain**: Career / Promotion
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: Quarterly (structured feedback) + Before each review cycle + On-demand (after major project, skip-level encounter)

---

## Purpose

Structure, solicit, and act on feedback from skip-level managers and senior leadership — converting sparse, high-signal feedback into actionable development priorities and building the relationships that produce calibration advocacy at the moment a promotion decision is made.

**What the EM does automatically**: For executive feedback, the EM:
1. Structures the feedback request to produce specific, usable signal (not "you're doing great")
2. Identifies the feedback sources most relevant to the promotion case
3. Synthesizes patterns across feedback instances into development priorities
4. Extracts the calibration talking points that executives are likely to use
5. Follows up in a way that demonstrates growth and builds the relationship

Most people get executive feedback wrong twice: they either don't ask for it, or they ask in a way that produces vague praise. The goal is not validation — it is information about how you are perceived at the level above, which is the only perception that matters for promotion.

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Feedback Source Prioritization

Not all feedback sources are equally useful for promotion. Prioritize by:

```
Calibration influence (weight 50%):
  Is this person in the room when your promotion is discussed?
  Primary: Your skip-level (almost always present or influential)
  High: Other EMs and Directors/VPs in calibration session
  Medium: Cross-functional VPs (may provide input if asked)
  Lower: Peers at your level (valuable, but not calibration decision-makers)

Feedback quality (weight 30%):
  Have they seen your work directly (not just heard about it)?
  Have they given you substantive feedback before (not just praise)?

Relationship warmth (weight 20%):
  Is this person likely to be direct with you?
  Have you interacted enough for them to have a real view?

Prioritize: High calibration influence + Have seen your work directly
```

### Step 2 — Feedback Request Structure

Structure feedback requests to produce specific, actionable signal:

**What NOT to ask:**
- "How am I doing?" → Produces social nicety, not career information
- "Do you think I'm ready for promotion?" → Forces a yes/no the person may not be ready to give
- "Any feedback for me?" → Produces whatever is comfortable, not what is useful

**What TO ask (by development dimension):**

For Scope / Strategic impact:
> "Looking at how I've been showing up on {{project/initiative}}, do you see me engaging at the right scope for someone targeting {{next level}}? Where do you see me thinking smaller than I should?"

For Ambiguity handling:
> "When {{situation requiring direction/decision}} came up, I took the approach of {{what you did}}. From the outside, how did that land? Would a {{target level}} have handled it differently?"

For Organizational leverage:
> "I've been focused on {{specific leverage activity}}. Do you notice that showing up in how the team operates? What would more impact look like from your seat?"

For Visibility / reputation:
> "When my name comes up in discussions at your level, what's the narrative? I'd rather know how I'm perceived than guess."

For Calibration preparation:
> "When {{review cycle}} comes around, what are the things you'd want to know about someone at my level who's targeting {{next level}}? I want to make sure I'm building toward the right things."

### Step 3 — Synthesize Feedback Patterns

```
After collecting feedback from 2+ sources, identify:

Consistent themes (across 2+ sources):
  → High-confidence signal: Act on this immediately
  
Single-source feedback:
  → Worth noting; test with a third source before major development pivot

Contradictions between sources:
  → Investigate the context difference: different sources see different things
  → Example: Skip-level says "more strategic"; manager says "already very strategic"
    → Likely a visibility gap: skip-level doesn't see the strategic work your manager does

Gap between self-perception and feedback:
  → This is the most valuable signal — it reveals blind spots
  → If feedback is worse than self-perception: don't explain/defend; ask "what would better look like?"
  → If feedback is better than self-perception: trust it; accelerate

Extractable calibration quote:
  → Any statement a senior leader made that could become a calibration talking point
  → Log in impact tracker with the source, context, and exact quote if possible
```

### Step 4 — The Feedback Loop

Feedback without follow-up is a one-time transaction. Feedback with follow-up builds a relationship:

```
30 days after receiving feedback:
  Share one specific action you took based on their input
  Framing: "After our conversation, I tried {{specific behavior change}} on {{project}}.
  The outcome was {{specific result}}. I wanted you to know your input had an effect."

Why this matters:
  1. Demonstrates that you are coachable and responsive
  2. Gives the feedback source a second observation point (they can now say "I gave them feedback on X and watched them apply it")
  3. Deepens the relationship — feedback turns into sponsorship
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the feedback synthesis reveal?

├── CONSISTENT "MORE STRATEGIC" FEEDBACK (2+ sources)
│   └── This is the most common feedback for L4→L5, M1→M2 candidates
│       It does NOT mean "think harder" — it means scope and framing
│       Translation: You are solving the problem given to you, not defining the problem
│       Action: Before next initiative, write a one-page problem framing
│       before receiving any spec or assignment
│       → Invoke: skills/career/promotion/leadership-growth.md — "defines the problem" behavior

├── CONSISTENT "NEEDS MORE VISIBILITY" FEEDBACK
│   └── Work quality is not the issue — reach and audience are
│       Translation: Work is happening; the right people don't know about it
│       → Invoke: skills/career/promotion/visibility-score.md for action plan

├── CONTRADICTORY FEEDBACK ACROSS SOURCES
│   └── Different audiences see different things — this is information, not confusion
│       Map the context: where is each source seeing you?
│       Example: "Strategic in 1:1s, execution-only in cross-team settings"
│         → The gap is in how you show up in a specific context
│         → Target that specific context for behavior change

├── POSITIVE FEEDBACK WITH NO SPECIFIC OBSERVATIONS
│   └── The person doesn't have enough visibility to give substantive feedback
│       This is a visibility gap, not a performance confirmation
│       Action: Create a visibility moment before asking again
│       → Invoke: skills/career/promotion/visibility-score.md

├── FEEDBACK SIGNIFICANTLY WORSE THAN SELF-PERCEPTION
│   └── This is the highest-value signal you can receive
│       Do NOT defend, explain, or dismiss
│       Ask exactly one follow-up question: "What would better look like?"
│       Then act on it and close the loop in 30 days
│       → Invoke: skills/career/promotion/promotion-gaps.md for recalibration

└── FEEDBACK CONFIRMS TRAJECTORY (you're on track)
    └── Extract the calibration quotes
        Log them in impact tracker with source and date
        Confirm: Will this person advocate in calibration? Ask directly if appropriate:
        "Would you be comfortable vouching for my {{target level}} readiness
        when calibration comes around?"
```

## Confidence Score

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Feedback from 2+ calibration-relevant sources | Base: 65% |
| Specific feedback questions used (not "how am I doing?") | +15% |
| Themes synthesized across sources | +10% |
| Calibration quotes extracted and logged | +10% |
| 30-day follow-up planned | +5% |
| Only manager feedback used | −25% |
| Feedback not synthesized (raw notes only) | −15% |
| No follow-up plan | −10% |

---

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Validation seeking** | "Do you think I'm ready?" instead of "Where do you see me thinking smaller than I should?" | Ask questions that produce information, not confirmation. |
| **No follow-through** | Feedback received; action taken; source never informed | Close the loop in 30 days. Feedback → action → report = sponsorship. |
| **One source** | All feedback from direct manager only | Calibration requires multiple voices. Seek skip-level and cross-functional senior feedback each cycle. |
| **Defending feedback** | Response to difficult feedback: "But here's why I did it that way..." | Never defend. Ask "What would better look like?" Information, not debate. |
| **Discarding positive feedback** | Strong feedback treated as politeness; never logged or used | Positive specific feedback is calibration ammunition. Log the exact words. |
