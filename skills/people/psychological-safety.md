# Skill: Psychological Safety Assessment

**Domain**: Team Health & People Leadership
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-coach.md`
**Cadence**: Quarterly + Immediate (after high-stress events/incidents)

---

## Purpose

Measure the degree to which team members feel safe to take risks, admit mistakes, and raise concerns without fear of negative consequences. Psychological safety is the #1 predictor of high-performing teams (Google Aristotle Project).

---

## The Four Stages of Psychological Safety (Timothy Clark)

1. **Inclusion Safety**: Do I feel welcomed and like I belong?
2. **Learner Safety**: Do I feel safe to ask questions, give/receive feedback, and make mistakes?
3. **Contributor Safety**: Do I feel safe to use my skills to make a full contribution?
4. **Challenger Safety**: Do I feel safe to challenge the status quo or point out risks?

---

## Analysis

### Step 1: Signal Detection
- **Silence**: Are meetings dominated by 1-2 voices?
- **Blame**: Do retrospectives focus on "who" instead of "why"?
- **Risk Aversion**: Does the team avoid ambitious goals or new technologies?

### Step 2: Surveying (Anonymous)
Use the Amy Edmondson 7-item scale:
1. If you make a mistake on this team, it is often held against you.
2. Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues.
3. People on this team sometimes reject others for being different.
4. It is safe to take a risk on this team.
5. It is difficult to ask other members of this team for help.
6. No one on this team would deliberately act in a way that undermines my efforts.
7. Working with members of this team, my unique skills and talents are valued and utilized.

### Step 3: Root Cause Analysis
- **Manager Behavior**: Is the EM modeling vulnerability?
- **Team Dynamics**: Is there a "brilliant jerk" suppressing others?
- **Org Culture**: Does the company reward "results at all costs"?

---

## Output Template

### 1. Safety Profile
- **Current Stage**: [1-4]
- **Holistic Score**: [0-100]
- **Key Signal Observed**: [e.g., "Silence in post-mortems"]

### 2. Intervention Plan
- **Vulnerability Modeling**: [e.g., EM shares their own recent mistake in standup]
- **Meeting Discipline**: [e.g., Implementing "Round Robin" feedback in planning]
- **Conflict Resolution**: [e.g., Facilitated 1:1 between Person A and B]

### 3. Ethical Note
Psychological safety is not about "being nice." It is about **candor**. A safe team is one where people can be brutally honest because they trust the intent of their peers.
