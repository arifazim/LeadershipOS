# Skill: Career Development

**Domain**: People Leadership Intelligence
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: Monthly (career conversation embedded in 1:1) + Quarterly (dedicated career session, 45–60 min)

---

## Purpose

Map each person's long-term career aspirations, close the gap between where they are and where they want to go, and identify the specific opportunities — inside and outside the current team — that will move them forward. Career development is distinct from growth plans (which address the near-term gap) and from promotion (which addresses the next level): it addresses the multi-year direction.

**What the EM does automatically**: In career development conversations, the EM:
1. Understands what each person actually wants — not what the EM thinks they should want
2. Distinguishes the IC track from the management track from specialist tracks, without bias toward either
3. Identifies the gap between current trajectory and long-term aspiration, including organizational reality
4. Creates opportunities the person couldn't create for themselves — introductions, visibility, stretch
5. Stays honest when the aspiration is not achievable in the current organization

**Ethical principle**: The EM who develops people primarily to serve the team's needs — and not the individual's interests — will eventually lose the best people. Genuine career development sometimes means helping someone leave for a better opportunity. An EM who is honest about what this role cannot offer, and who helps the person find what they need, earns reputation and trust that outlasts any single team. Holding people back to protect team stability is a misuse of the managerial relationship.

---

## Inputs

### Required
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Person's stated aspirations** | Where they want to be in 2–5 years — in their words |
| **Current trajectory** | Where their current work is taking them on autopilot, absent any intervention |
| **Organizational opportunities** | What paths, roles, and experiences are actually available within this org |

### Optional
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Prior career conversations** | What was explored before; what changed; what the person moved toward or away from |
| **Their values and motivators** | What matters most to them in their work — autonomy, mastery, purpose, stability, influence |
| **External context** | What opportunities exist in the market; what the person knows about or is curious about |

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Map the Career Aspiration

```
Three aspiration horizons:

1-year horizon (near-term): "Where do you want to be in your career a year from now?"
  Focus: Level, role, type of work, scope
  Useful because: Concrete enough to plan toward; connects directly to current growth plan

3-year horizon (medium-term): "In three years, what kind of work do you want to be doing?"
  Focus: Domain expertise, organizational level, impact type, team structure
  Useful because: Requires the EM to think about paths, not just tasks

5-year horizon (long-term): "When you imagine a job you'd find deeply satisfying in five years, what does it look like?"
  Focus: Purpose, lifestyle, type of problems, type of environment
  Useful because: Often reveals values and constraints the person hasn't articulated

Career aspiration types:

IC growth track (depth-focused):
  The person wants to go deep in technical expertise; become a principal or staff engineer;
  build systems that outlast any product cycle; be the person others come to for technical judgment
  Path: L4 → L5 → L6 → Principal/Staff; requires platform-level impact and technical influence

Management track (breadth-focused):
  The person wants to lead a team; develop others; shape organizational direction;
  work with people as much as or more than code
  Path: IC → TL → EM; requires demonstrated leadership behaviors, not just seniority

Specialist track (mastery-focused):
  Deep expertise in a specific domain: security, ML infrastructure, platform reliability,
  developer experience; they want to be the best in the organization (or industry) at one thing
  Path: Often lateral before deepening; requires organizational positioning around the specialty

Founder/entrepreneurial track:
  The person wants to build something — a product, a company, or an initiative from scratch
  They are drawn to early-stage, ambiguous, zero-to-one work
  Path: Requires increasing ownership experience, startup-adjacent exposure, product collaboration
  The EM's role: Create zero-to-one opportunities; be honest about the ceiling in a mature org

Pivoting track:
  The person wants to move to a different function — from engineering to product, design, data,
  or technical program management
  Path: Usually requires a transition plan, sponsorship, and a receiving opportunity
  The EM's role: Support the pivot honestly; don't hold them back to preserve headcount
```

### Step 2 — Assess the Gap Between Aspiration and Current Trajectory

```
Current trajectory assessment:
  "If this person stays on their current path for three more years, where will they end up?"
  Consider: Their growth rate, the team's growth rate, the available scope in the org

Trajectory gap types:

Gap 1 — Scope ceiling
  Current org doesn't have a role or scope large enough for the person's aspiration
  Signal: They aspire to Staff/Principal but the org has no staff track; or they want to
          lead multiple teams but no such role exists
  EM response: Be honest about the ceiling. "To get to where you want to be, you may need
  a role that doesn't exist here yet. Here's what I can help you build toward while you're here —
  and here's what I'd recommend when the ceiling becomes the limiting factor."

Gap 2 — Pace gap
  The org can get them there, but not at the pace the person needs
  Signal: They want to be an EM in 18 months; the realistic path is 3+ years
  EM response: Set honest expectations while accelerating what can be accelerated
  "I want to be honest about timeline. The realistic path from here is {{N}} years if everything
  goes well. Here's what would need to happen for it to be faster."

Gap 3 — Track mismatch
  The person is on one track but aspires to another
  Signal: Strong IC aspiring to management; high-EQ engineer pushed into IC work
  EM response: Provide honest assessment of fit for the desired track
  "I want to give you an honest view of what that track requires and whether I see
  you succeeding there. Can I share that?"

Gap 4 — Hidden aspiration
  The person gives a safe answer ("I just want to grow in my current role") that doesn't
  reflect what they actually want
  Signal: Disconnection between their stated aspiration and their energy, curiosity, or the
          projects they get excited about
  EM response: Create space for the honest aspiration
  "I want to ask you a different question. If you could do any job — inside or outside engineering —
  what would you actually want to do?"
```

### Step 3 — Map Available Opportunities

```
Opportunity types the EM can create or surface:

Organizational opportunities:
  — Open roles in adjacent teams that advance the person's trajectory
  — Internal transfers that build the skill they need
  — High-visibility projects that create executive exposure
  — Cross-functional initiatives that build the breadth aspired to

Learning opportunities:
  — Senior engineers or leaders the EM can introduce them to
  — Conferences, communities, or professional groups in their domain
  — Sponsorship from skip-level or cross-functional leaders

Leadership opportunities:
  — Tech lead role on an upcoming initiative
  — Mentoring a junior engineer
  — Representing engineering in cross-functional planning
  — Running a team process (retrospective, on-call, architecture review)

Market opportunities (external):
  When the internal path is not available or is too slow:
  The EM who helps someone find the right external opportunity earns permanent loyalty
  "I want to help you get where you're going. If that's not here, I'd rather you leave
  with my support than leave because we failed you."

Opportunity gap:
  If the team cannot provide the opportunities needed for the person's growth within 6 months:
  → Be transparent: "I can't create the {{opportunity}} you need here. Here's what I can do:
  {{what's possible}}. For the rest, I'd recommend {{external path}}."
```

### Step 4 — Build the Career Development Plan

```
Career development plan (quarterly, ~45 min):

Part 1: Aspiration check (10 min)
  "Has your thinking about where you want to go changed since we last talked?"
  Aspirations shift — especially at major life events, after external conversations, after feedback
  Do not assume the aspiration from 6 months ago is still current

Part 2: Trajectory assessment (15 min)
  "Based on the last quarter, are you moving toward your goal? What's working?"
  "What's slowing you down?"
  Name the gap type: scope ceiling, pace gap, track mismatch, or hidden aspiration

Part 3: Opportunity design (15 min)
  "Here's what I see as available for you in the next quarter: {{opportunities}}"
  "Is there something I could help you access that I'm not thinking of?"
  Commit: "I'll make the introduction to {{person}} by {{date}}"

Part 4: Sponsor commitment (5 min)
  "I want to actively advocate for you. For me to do that well, I need to know what
  you want people to know about you. What's the story you want me to tell?"
  → Sponsorship is different from mentorship:
    Mentorship: "Here's my advice"
    Sponsorship: "Here's what I said about you in the room where you weren't present"
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the career development assessment reveal?

├── ASPIRATION AND CURRENT TRAJECTORY ARE ALIGNED
│   └── Accelerate: create the next opportunity before they plateau
│       Check for hidden aspirations: "Is this really where you want to go,
│       or is this the safe answer?"
│       Refresh the plan quarterly; don't let it become routine

├── SCOPE CEILING DETECTED (ORG CAN'T DELIVER WHAT THEY NEED)
│   └── Be honest — immediately
│       "I want to be transparent. The role you're describing doesn't exist here,
│       and I can't guarantee it will. Here's what I can offer: {{realistic path}}.
│       Is that enough to make this the right place for now?"
│       Help them think through options without pressuring them to stay or go

├── MANAGEMENT TRACK ASPIRATION (IC CURRENTLY)
│   └── Assess leadership readiness honestly — not by promoting, but by creating
│       tech lead experiences, mentoring responsibilities, and people management exposure
│       "Let's test this. Lead the team through the next sprint planning.
│       Let's see how you feel about it and what I observe."
│       Avoid: "You'd be a great manager" without concrete experiences to test it

├── PIVOT ASPIRATION (TO DIFFERENT FUNCTION)
│   └── Support it — do not hold them back
│       "Tell me more about what draws you to {{function}}. Let's figure out
│       how to build the path toward it."
│       Practical: Identify the skills they need; create cross-functional exposure now
│       Connect them to people in the target function
│       Be ready for the conversation about their timeline to transition

├── HIDDEN ASPIRATION SUSPECTED
│   └── Open the real conversation: "I notice you get excited about {{thing}}.
│       If you could design your ideal role, what would it look like?"
│       Don't push — plant the question and let them come back to it
│       Return to it in the next session if they deflect

└── ATTRITION RISK DUE TO CAREER STAGNATION
    └── This is urgent — the career conversation is the retention lever
        "I want to understand: is there a version of your career here that you're excited about?
        What would need to be true?"
        → Invoke: skills/people/engagement.md for retention protocol
        → Invoke: skills/people/motivation.md for what is currently motivating them
```

## Confidence Score

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Aspiration mapped across three horizons | Base: 65% |
| Gap type identified (not just "they need to grow") | +15% |
| Specific opportunities committed with dates | +10% |
| Sponsorship conversation had | +10% |
| Career org ceiling assessed honestly | +5% |
| Career conversation not held in 3+ months | −25% |
| Aspirations assumed rather than asked | −15% |
| Person held back to protect team (career stagnation enabled) | −40% (disqualifying) |

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Assumed aspiration** | EM designs a career path without asking what the person actually wants | "Where do you want to go?" is the required first question. Every time. Aspirations change. |
| **Org ceiling avoided** | Person aspires to staff/principal; role doesn't exist; EM says nothing | Name the ceiling honestly. Helping them see their options clearly is more respectful than false hope. |
| **Sponsorship as mentorship** | EM gives advice in 1:1; never advocates in rooms where the person isn't present | Sponsorship is what happens outside the 1:1. Ask: "What did I say about this person last week in a meeting they weren't in?" |
| **Track bias** | EM steers high performers toward management without asking if that's what they want | Both tracks are valid. The choice is the person's. Not every strong IC should become a manager. |
| **Holding people back** | Person is ready to move but EM delays because they don't want to lose them | Great managers develop people who leave. This is the job. |
