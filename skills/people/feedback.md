# Skill: Feedback

**Domain**: People Leadership Intelligence
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: Continuous (not reserved for review cycles) — the EM gives and receives feedback as a consistent practice

---

## Purpose

Deliver feedback that is specific, timely, and behavior-focused — and build a team culture where feedback flows in all directions, including upward. Feedback is the primary mechanism for calibrating performance before it becomes a problem and for accelerating growth beyond natural pace.

**What the EM does automatically**: In the feedback practice, the EM:
1. Gives developmental feedback within 48–72 hours of the triggering behavior — not months later
2. Uses the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) framework to ensure feedback is observable and non-judgmental
3. Delivers positive feedback with the same specificity as developmental feedback
4. Proactively creates the conditions to receive feedback from their team
5. Tracks patterns — a single feedback event is a data point; a repeated pattern is a development need

**Ethical principle**: Feedback withheld is a disservice. The EM who softens developmental feedback to the point of inaudibility, or who saves it for the formal review, is not being kind — they are protecting their own comfort at the expense of the other person's growth. Specific, timely, honest feedback is a form of respect. Vague feedback treats the recipient as too fragile to hear the truth.

## Inputs

### Required
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Triggering event** | The specific situation or behavior that prompted the feedback need |
| **Observable behavior** | What was actually done or said — not the interpretation of it |
| **Impact** | The specific effect the behavior had on the work, the team, or the relationship |

### Optional
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pattern context** | Whether this is a first instance or a recurring behavior |
| **Person's self-awareness** | Whether they likely know this is feedback-worthy or it will be a surprise |
| **Relationship context** | Trust level, recent history, emotional state of the person |

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Apply the SBI Framework

```
SBI = Situation → Behavior → Impact

S — Situation
  The specific context in which the behavior occurred
  Purpose: Anchor the feedback in an observable moment; prevents "you always do this"
  Format: "In {{specific situation}}..."
  Example: "In the sprint planning meeting on Tuesday..."
  
  Trap: Being too vague — "Sometimes in meetings..." loses the specificity that makes feedback land
  Trap: Generalizing — "You always..." escalates defensiveness and is rarely accurate

B — Behavior
  The specific action or words observed — not the interpretation
  Purpose: Keep the feedback to what was observable; the person cannot argue with what they did
  Format: "...you {{specific behavior}}..."
  Example: "...you interrupted {{person}} twice before they finished their point..."
  
  Trap: "You were dismissive" — that's an interpretation; "You cut off their sentence and moved on" is behavior
  Trap: "You seemed frustrated" — inference; "You raised your voice and left the room" is behavior

I — Impact
  The specific effect on the work, the team, the relationship, or the outcome
  Purpose: Connects the behavior to why it matters; prevents "so what?" defensiveness
  Format: "...and the impact was {{specific consequence}}."
  Example: "...and the impact was that their idea wasn't heard, and they haven't contributed
  in any team forum since."
  
  Trap: Generic impact — "It wasn't great for team morale" is too vague to be actionable
  Impact types:
    On the work: "The design review decision was made without complete information"
    On the team: "Two engineers mentioned they feel less safe speaking up in planning"
    On the relationship: "The client asked me directly whether they can trust our estimates"
    On the person's reputation: "Your manager's peer has started routing around you"
```

### Step 2 — Classify Feedback Type and Calibrate Delivery

```
Developmental feedback (the behavior should change):
  Trigger threshold: One serious instance, or the same minor behavior 2–3 times
  Timing: Within 48–72 hours of the triggering event — memory and context are fresh
  Private: Developmental feedback is always delivered privately unless the behavior occurred publicly
           and others witnessed it and are affected by not seeing it addressed
  Format: SBI + forward ask + space for their response
  
  After SBI: "Going forward, what I'm asking for is {{specific behavior change}}."
  Then: "What's your reaction to this?"
  Critical: The forward ask must be specific and behavioral:
    ✓ "When someone is mid-sentence in a meeting, wait until they've finished before responding"
    ✗ "Be more respectful in meetings" — not specific enough to know when it's been achieved

Positive feedback (the behavior should be reinforced and repeated):
  The most neglected category of feedback in engineering teams
  Trigger: Any behavior above baseline that represents the person operating at their best
  Timing: Within 24 hours — the behavior is still vivid and the reinforcement has maximum effect
  Format: SBI — same framework, no softening needed
  Trap: "Great job on that!" — not feedback; it's a compliment; the person doesn't know what to repeat
  Better: "In the incident call yesterday, you stayed completely calm when the CEO joined unexpectedly
          and you walked him through the status in plain language — the impact was that he left
          satisfied instead of alarmed. That's exactly how I want to see you show up in high-stakes situations."

Patterns vs. incidents:
  First occurrence: Note it; give feedback; give the benefit of the doubt
  Second occurrence: "This is the second time I've seen this. I want to make sure I wasn't unclear."
  Third occurrence: This is a pattern; the feedback must be more direct:
    "We've talked about this twice now. This is the third instance. 
    I need to understand what's getting in the way of this changing."
  Fourth occurrence: This is now a performance issue
    → Invoke: skills/people/performance-review.md for performance management framing
```

### Step 3 — Deliver the Feedback Conversation

```
Feedback conversation structure:

Step 1: Ask permission (especially for developmental feedback)
  "I want to share some feedback with you. Is now a good time?"
  Why: Consent increases receptivity; springing feedback during an unrelated conversation
  can create defensiveness because the person is in the wrong cognitive mode

Step 2: Deliver SBI — cleanly and specifically
  No hedging: "I might be wrong, but I thought maybe..."
  No sandwich: Positive-negative-positive softens the feedback into incoherence
  Just: "In {{situation}}, you {{behavior}}, and the impact was {{impact}}."

Step 3: Forward ask
  "Going forward, here's what I'm asking for: {{specific behavior}}"
  The forward ask is not a criticism of what happened — it is a description of what comes next

Step 4: Invite their response
  "What's your reaction to this?"
  Then: Listen. They may have context you don't.
  If they have context that changes the picture: update the feedback
  "That's helpful — I hadn't seen that part. Given that, here's what I'd still ask..."
  If they become defensive: don't rescue the feedback by softening it
  "I understand this is hard to hear. I want to sit with your reaction for a moment."

Step 5: Close with clarity
  Confirm the shared understanding: "So what I'm asking for going forward is {{behavior}}.
  Does that feel clear?"
  If the pattern issue: "I want to check back in on this in {{2 weeks}}. Is that okay?"
```

### Step 4 — Receive Feedback as the EM

```
The EM who does not actively receive feedback creates a one-directional culture.
If the team sees the EM give feedback but never ask for it, feedback becomes a tool
of authority, not a shared practice.

Creating conditions for upward feedback:
  In 1:1s: "What's one thing I could do differently to be more helpful to you?"
  In retrospectives: "What's one thing I could have done better this sprint?"
  Explicitly: "I want this team to be able to tell me when I'm getting something wrong.
  Can you tell me about a time recently where you wished I had handled something differently?"

Receiving feedback well:
  Listen without defending: The instinct to explain or justify is natural and almost always wrong
  "Thank you for telling me that" is always the right first response
  Ask clarifying questions: "Can you give me an example?" — not to disprove, to understand
  Act on it: Feedback received and not acted on is worse than not asking — it signals the feedback was theater
  Report back: "You told me two weeks ago that I was too directive in architecture reviews.
  I've tried to ask more questions instead. Has that been better?"

The EM who cannot receive feedback about their own style cannot credibly ask for it from others.
```

### Step 5 — Build a Feedback Culture

```
Feedback culture indicators (team-level):

Healthy:
  Peer feedback is given directly and not exclusively through the manager
  Engineers ask for feedback proactively after significant work
  Retrospective feedback is specific and forward-looking, not a complaints session
  Engineers can tell the manager when something isn't working without fear

Unhealthy:
  Feedback only travels downward (manager → engineer)
  Engineers route feedback about peers through the manager
  Retrospective feedback is vague or avoidant
  Engineers are surprised by their performance review content

Practices that build feedback culture:
  Model it first: give specific feedback publicly when appropriate (positive feedback especially)
  Name it when you receive feedback well: "I want to be transparent — {{person}} told me
  {{feedback}} and I'm working on it. I appreciate that they were direct with me."
  React visibly to feedback: Update behavior; reference the feedback; show that it mattered
  Protect the person who gave difficult feedback: do not behave differently toward them
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the feedback situation require?

├── FIRST INSTANCE, MINOR BEHAVIOR
│   └── Give feedback within 48–72 hours
│       SBI format; forward ask; note the instance in your records
│       Tone: Curious and direct, not corrective
│       "I wanted to flag this quickly so it doesn't become a pattern."

├── SECOND INSTANCE OF SAME BEHAVIOR
│   └── Give feedback again; name the pattern
│       "This is the second time I've noticed this — I want to check if my earlier
│       feedback was clear. Here's what I saw and what I'm still asking for."
│       Document: Two instances creates a pattern record

├── THIRD+ INSTANCE — PATTERN ESTABLISHED
│   └── This is now a performance issue, not just a feedback issue
│       Direct: "We've talked about this before. This is the third instance.
│       I need to understand what's getting in the way of this changing."
│       → Invoke: skills/people/performance-review.md for performance management framing

├── PERSON IS DEFENSIVE — SHUTS DOWN OR PUSHES BACK
│   └── Do NOT rescue by softening the feedback
│       "I understand this is hard to hear. I'd like to give you a moment to respond."
│       If they dispute the facts: "Tell me what you saw. I want to make sure I have
│       the full picture." Update if warranted; hold the feedback if not.
│       Follow up: "I want to give you some time to sit with this. Can we talk again
│       in a few days after you've had some space?"

├── PATTERN OF AVOIDING GIVING FEEDBACK
│   └── Avoidance is a leadership failure — name it to yourself first
│       Ask: "What's making it hard for me to give this feedback?"
│         — Fear of the reaction? Fear of damaging the relationship?
│         — Uncertainty whether it's fair?
│       If uncertainty: Gather one more data point first; then give the feedback
│       If fear: Give the feedback anyway. Avoidance compounds the problem.

└── EM RECEIVING CRITICAL FEEDBACK
    └── Don't defend; don't diminish; don't dismiss
        "Thank you for telling me that. This is helpful."
        Follow with: "Can you give me a specific example so I understand what you're observing?"
        Act on it within two weeks; report back to the person who gave it
```

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Feedback sandwich** | Positive-feedback-positive; developmental feedback disappears in the middle | Don't sandwich. SBI directly. The person doesn't remember the middle. |
| **Saved for review** | Developmental feedback first surfaced in a formal review | Give feedback within 72 hours. Reviews should contain no surprises. |
| **Vague impact** | "It wasn't ideal for team morale" | Name the specific consequence: who was affected, how, what changed as a result. |
| **Interpretation as behavior** | "You were passive-aggressive in that meeting" | Name what was observed: "You nodded when asked if you agreed, then sent an email opposing the decision." |
| **Rescuing from defensiveness** | Person pushes back; EM softens the feedback until it disappears | Hold the feedback. "I understand you see it differently. What I'm still asking for is {{behavior}}." |
