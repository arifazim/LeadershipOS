# Skill: One-on-One

**Domain**: People Leadership Intelligence
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: Weekly or biweekly (per individual) — the EM's most critical recurring people practice

---

## Purpose

Run one-on-one meetings that serve the other person — not the EM's status-tracking needs — by adapting the meeting mode to what the person needs that week, detecting signals that don't appear on Slack or in standups, and building the trust necessary for honest conversation before a crisis makes honesty urgent.

**What the EM does automatically**: In every 1:1 cadence, the EM:
1. Prepares by reviewing prior themes, open commitments, and what has happened since the last meeting
2. Reads the person's energy and adjusts the meeting mode accordingly
3. Listens for signals: burnout, disengagement, ambition, friction, and flight risk
4. Tracks themes across multiple 1:1s — patterns matter more than single sessions
5. Ensures the person drives the agenda, not the EM

**Ethical principle**: A 1:1 is the person's meeting, not the manager's. The EM who turns every 1:1 into a status check has converted a relationship-building tool into a reporting mechanism. The EM learns more by listening than by talking. Information extracted under authority is not the same as information shared in trust. Build the relationship before the crisis arrives.

---

## Inputs

### Required
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Person's current context** | What project or situation they're in this week; recent wins and recent friction |
| **Prior 1:1 themes** | What has come up across the last 3–4 sessions; open commitments from the EM |
| **Engagement signals** | Observable changes in energy, output quality, collaboration, or mood since last meeting |

### Optional
| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Recent feedback received** | What the person has heard from others; how they received it |
| **Career development status** | Where they are in a growth plan or promotion track |
| **Organizational context** | Reorgs, team changes, roadmap shifts that may be affecting this person |

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Select the Meeting Mode

Not every 1:1 should have the same format. The right mode depends on what the person needs:

```
Mode 1: Open / Unstructured
  When: Default mode; person owns the agenda
  Structure: "What's on your mind?" → follow their lead
  EM role: Ask questions; listen; do not fill silence with updates
  Trap to avoid: Defaulting to project status when the person goes quiet

Mode 2: Check-in / Reconnect
  When: After a difficult sprint, incident, reorg, or interpersonal tension
  Structure: "How are you actually doing?" before any work topics
  EM role: Make space for the emotional reality before the operational one
  Signal this is needed: Person seems different than their baseline; energy is off

Mode 3: Coaching
  When: Person is working through a challenge, decision, or skill development
  Structure: GROW (Goal → Reality → Options → Will) — see skills/people/coaching.md
  EM role: Ask questions; do not solve; the person must own the conclusion
  Trap to avoid: Giving the answer because it's faster; this forfeits the coaching

Mode 4: Career / Growth
  When: Monthly (minimum) or when person initiates; never skip this mode indefinitely
  Structure: Where they want to go → what's required → what's the gap → what's next
  EM role: Honest assessment + specific opportunities + follow-through
  Trap to avoid: Generic encouragement without a concrete plan

Mode 5: Feedback
  When: A specific behavior or situation warrants direct feedback
  Structure: Situation → Behavior → Impact → Forward ask
  EM role: Specific, direct, timely; not wrapped in so much softening it becomes unclear
  → Invoke: skills/people/feedback.md for preparation

Mode 6: Performance / Accountability
  When: A commitment was missed; a behavior pattern requires naming
  Structure: Fact → impact → expectation → agreement → timeline
  EM role: Direct; not punitive; not saved for formal reviews
  Trap to avoid: Waiting for the formal review to have the performance conversation
```

### Step 2 — Prepare the 1:1

```
Five-minute preparation before every 1:1:

1. Review prior session notes
   What themes have been coming up? What did I commit to? What did they commit to?
   
2. Check observable signals
   How has their output, energy, and collaboration looked this week?
   Anything that diverges from their baseline?

3. Name the mode
   What does this person most need from this 1:1 today?

4. Prepare two to three questions
   Not an agenda to get through — questions that could open the conversation in the right direction
   
5. Clear the competing priorities
   A distracted manager wastes the meeting. Be present.

Questions by mode:

  Open: "What's most on your mind right now?" | "What do you want to make sure we talk about?"
  Check-in: "How are you doing beyond the work?" | "What's the hardest part of things right now?"
  Coaching: "What would success look like?" | "What have you tried?" | "What's stopping you?"
  Career: "Where do you want to be in two years?" | "What kind of work energizes you most?"
  Feedback: "I want to share something I've been observing — is now a good time?"
  Performance: "Can you walk me through what happened with {{commitment}}?"
```

### Step 3 — Detect Signals During the 1:1

```
Signals that require attention beyond the current session:

Engagement signals:
  Decreasing: Shorter answers, less initiative, work feels like obligation
  "We used to talk about the architecture and now they just say it's fine"
  → Invoke: skills/people/engagement.md

Burnout signals:
  Exhaustion language, cynicism about the team/company, reduced quality of work
  "Everything is fine" when the sprint was clearly brutal
  → Invoke: skills/people/burnout.md

Career plateau signals:
  No excitement about the work, not asking about growth, mention of external conversations
  → Invoke: skills/people/career-development.md

Flight risk signals:
  Complaints that have been surfaced repeatedly without resolution
  Disengagement from team events, peer relationships cooling
  LinkedIn activity increasing (if visible), resume-quality questions
  → Immediate action: have the retention conversation this session or the next one

Interpersonal conflict signals:
  Avoidance of specific names, frustration with collaboration, "it's just easier to do it myself"
  → Invoke: skills/conflict/conflict-diagnosis.md

Promotion readiness signals:
  Taking on work above their level, asking about what comes next, peer recognition
  → Invoke: skills/career/promotion/promotion-readiness.md

Structural signals (not about the person):
  Repeated friction in the same area across multiple 1:1s = structural problem, not individual
  "I keep running into the same problem with the API integration" (3rd time)
  → Invoke: skills/organizational/conflict-prediction.md
```

### Step 4 — Track Themes Over Time

```
A single 1:1 is a snapshot. Themes across multiple sessions are the signal.

Theme tracking:
  Maintain a running log per person with these categories:
  — Energy pattern: increasing, stable, decreasing over the last 4 sessions
  — Open themes: topics that recurred but weren't resolved
  — EM commitments: what the EM promised to do; whether it was done
  — Their commitments: what they agreed to try; what happened
  — Career thread: where the career conversation is and what was last agreed

Pattern recognition:
  Same problem mentioned twice: flag for follow-up
  Same problem mentioned three times: it hasn't been resolved and is now a theme
  Same problem mentioned four times: the EM has not addressed it — this is the EM's failure

EM commitment tracking is non-negotiable:
  Nothing breaks 1:1 trust faster than the EM not following through on what they said they'd do
  If the EM said "I'll look into X" — open the prior notes before every 1:1
  If the commitment was not fulfilled: name it before they bring it up
  "I haven't gotten back to you on X yet. Here's where I am and when you can expect it."
```

### Step 5 — End the 1:1 With Clarity

```
Every 1:1 should end with clarity on:

1. One thing the EM is taking away: "The most important thing I heard today is..."
2. One thing the person is taking forward: Not assigned — emerged from the conversation
3. Any open commitments: Named by both parties; tracked by both parties
4. Next mode: What does the next 1:1 need to look like?

Closing questions:
  "What do you need from me between now and our next conversation?"
  "Is there anything you want to make sure I took away from today?"
  "What's the one thing that would make your next two weeks easier?"
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the current 1:1 pattern reveal?

├── PERSON ALWAYS HAS NOTHING TO TALK ABOUT
│   └── Two possible causes:
│       A: The 1:1 has become a status meeting — person doesn't see it as their space
│       Fix A: Reset the format; give explicit permission for any topic; hold silence
│       B: They are disengaged — nothing at work matters enough to bring
│       Fix B: → Invoke: skills/people/engagement.md
│       Do NOT: Fill the silence with project updates — that confirms it's a status meeting

├── 1:1 REVEALS BURNOUT SIGNAL
│   └── Do not finish the planned topics
│       Make this session about their wellbeing
│       "I want to set aside the work topics. How are you holding up?"
│       → Invoke: skills/people/burnout.md for full protocol

├── PERSON BRINGS THE SAME PROBLEM REPEATEDLY
│   └── This is the EM's failure to act, not the person's failure to move on
│       Name it: "We've talked about this three times. I haven't fixed the underlying issue.
│       Let me be specific about what I'm going to do and when."
│       Structural problem: → Invoke skills/organizational/decision-network.md or conflict-prediction.md

├── FLIGHT RISK DETECTED
│   └── Do not save the retention conversation for the next 1:1
│       This session: "I want to be direct — I want you here. What would make this the
│       right place for you to be for the next two years?"
│       After the session: → Invoke: skills/people/engagement.md for retention protocol

├── CAREER CONVERSATION HASN'T HAPPENED IN 6+ WEEKS
│   └── Reprioritize — career conversations cannot be deferred indefinitely
│       People disengage when they believe their manager doesn't care about their growth
│       Next 1:1: Explicit career mode; no other topics unless they're urgent

└── 1:1S ARE HEALTHY — PERSON ENGAGED, TRUST STRONG
    └── Maintain cadence; don't let a busy sprint cause cancellations
        The 1:1 cancelled when things are busiest is when it's most needed
        Continue tracking themes; refresh the career conversation monthly
```

## Confidence Score

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Meeting mode selected intentionally | Base: 65% |
| Prior themes reviewed before session | +15% |
| Person drove the agenda | +10% |
| Signals tracked across sessions | +10% |
| EM commitments fulfilled | +5% |
| EM turned the 1:1 into a status meeting | −30% |
| Career conversation skipped for 6+ weeks | −15% |
| Flight risk detected and not acted on | −25% (critical gap) |

---

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Status meeting conversion** | Every 1:1 begins with "so how is {{project}} going?" | Give the person the first 10 minutes with no agenda. "What do you want to talk about?" |
| **EM talks more than the person** | The person leaves feeling they were briefed, not heard | 70% listening, 30% talking. If you said more than they did, it was the wrong meeting. |
| **Career conversation deferred** | "We'll talk about that next time" for 3 months | Career conversations are not optional; they are the primary retention tool. |
| **EM commitments forgotten** | Person brings up something the EM agreed to do 3 weeks ago; EM has no memory | Review prior notes before every 1:1. Nothing breaks trust faster than forgotten commitments. |
| **Signals ignored** | Burnout or flight risk signals noticed, not acted on; person leaves 6 weeks later | Signals require action in the next 48 hours, not the next 1:1. |
