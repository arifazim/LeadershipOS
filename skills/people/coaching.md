# Skill: Coaching

**Domain**: People Leadership Intelligence
**Owner**: `subagents/engineering-manager.md`
**Cadence**: On-demand within 1:1s — and explicitly for monthly developmental sessions with each direct report

---

## Purpose

Develop people by asking rather than telling — creating the conditions for insight, capability, and ownership that cannot emerge when the manager always provides the answer. Coaching is the primary tool for developing engineers who can operate beyond their current level; it is distinct from mentoring (sharing experience) and managing (directing work).

**What the EM does automatically**: In coaching conversations, the EM:
1. Assesses the person's coaching readiness — whether they need coaching, direction, or support
2. Uses the GROW model to structure the conversation without imposing the destination
3. Holds the discomfort of silence and uncertainty rather than rescuing with an answer
4. Tracks progress across sessions — coaching with no continuity is conversation, not development
5. Distinguishes coaching from therapy: stays focused on work, goals, and behavior; not diagnosis

**Ethical principle**: Coaching is a gift of time and attention — not a tool for compliance. The EM who uses coaching as a mechanism to arrive at the answer they already had in mind is not coaching; they are leading a witness. Genuine coaching holds open the possibility that the coachee's conclusion might be better than the manager's. If the EM cannot tolerate a conclusion they disagree with, they should give direction, not coaching.

## Analysis

### Step 1 — Assess Coaching Readiness

Not every situation calls for coaching. Situational assessment prevents applying the wrong approach:

```
Four leadership modes (situational leadership):

Mode A — Direction (Low competence, Low commitment)
  Situation: Person is new to the task; doesn't know how to proceed
  Right approach: Tell them what to do; show them how; explain why
  Wrong approach: Coaching (they don't have the foundation to generate insight yet)
  Signal: "I don't know where to start" | "What should I do here?"

Mode B — Coaching (Low-medium competence, Variable commitment)
  Situation: Person has some capability but needs to develop further; needs support
  Right approach: GROW framework; ask-don't-tell; hold space for their conclusions
  Signal: "I tried X but it didn't work" | "I'm not sure this approach is right"

Mode C — Support (High competence, Low commitment)
  Situation: Person can do it but lacks motivation or confidence
  Right approach: Affirm their ability; explore what's in the way; don't take the task back
  Signal: "I know what to do, I just..." | Capability without action
  Wrong approach: More coaching — they need encouragement, not questions

Mode D — Delegation (High competence, High commitment)
  Situation: Person is capable and motivated; add responsibility, not coaching
  Right approach: Set the outcome; stay out of the way; check in at milestones
  Wrong approach: Over-coaching a capable, motivated person — it signals distrust

Coaching is appropriate when: Mode B or C
Coaching is NOT appropriate when: Person needs direction (A) or space (D)
```

### Step 2 — Apply the GROW Model

```
GROW = Goal → Reality → Options → Will

Phase G — Goal (5–10 min)
  Purpose: Establish what the person wants to achieve from this conversation
  Key question: "What would success look like for you at the end of this conversation?"
  Secondary questions:
    "What do you want to be different as a result of our discussion?"
    "What is the specific thing you want to work through today?"
  
  EM trap: Setting the goal for them ("Let's talk about your communication")
  Instead: "What's most on your mind about your development right now?"
  
  If the goal is vague ("I want to be better at stakeholder management"):
  "What would better look like in practice? Can you give me a specific situation?"

Phase R — Reality (10–15 min)
  Purpose: Understand the current situation as the person experiences it — not as the EM sees it
  Key questions:
    "What's happening right now?"
    "What have you already tried?"
    "What's working and what isn't?"
    "What do you think is in your way?"
  
  EM trap: Diagnosing the reality for them; saying "The real issue is..."
  Instead: Hold space for their diagnosis; ask clarifying questions
  
  Powerful reality question: "What part of this is within your control?"
  This separates what they can act on from what they cannot.

Phase O — Options (10–15 min)
  Purpose: Generate possibilities without evaluating — evaluation shuts down generation
  Key questions:
    "What options do you have?"
    "What else could you try?"
    "What would you do if you couldn't fail?"
    "What would you advise someone else in your situation?"
    "What haven't you considered yet?"
  
  EM trap: Proposing their own option first, which anchors the conversation
  Instead: Wait until they have exhausted their options before adding any
  
  If they run out of options: "Would you like me to share some things others have tried?"
  Always ask permission before offering; it preserves their ownership of the solution

Phase W — Will (5–10 min)
  Purpose: Convert insight into commitment — the most skipped phase; the most important
  Key questions:
    "What are you going to do?"
    "When will you do it?"
    "What might get in the way, and how will you handle it?"
    "What support do you need from me?"
    "On a scale of 1–10, how committed are you to doing this?"
  
  If commitment score is below 7: "What would need to be true to get to a 7?"
  Low commitment = wrong option was chosen, or the goal wasn't theirs
  
  EM trap: Accepting vague commitment ("I'll think about it")
  Instead: Specific action + specific timeline + specific check-in
```

### Step 3 — Coaching Technique Library

```
Powerful questions by phase:

Goal-setting questions:
  "What's the most important thing you could work on right now?"
  "If we solve this today, what opens up for you?"
  "What's the version of this you haven't dared to name yet?"

Reality questions:
  "What have you assumed that might not be true?"
  "What are you tolerating that you shouldn't be?"
  "If this were someone else's problem, what would you notice?"

Options questions:
  "What's the bravest thing you could do here?"
  "What would you do with twice the resources?"
  "What would you do if your manager weren't watching?"
  "What would you regret not trying?"

Will questions:
  "What's the first tiny step you could take today?"
  "Who needs to know about this commitment?"
  "What does accountability look like for you?"

Reflecting back (non-evaluative):
  "What I'm hearing is... Is that right?"
  "You said X earlier and now you're saying Y — tell me more about that."
  "That sounds important — say more about it."

Silence:
  After asking a powerful question: count to 10 before speaking.
  The answer that comes after a long silence is usually the real answer.
  The EM who rescues from silence loses the insight.
```

### Step 4 — Track Coaching Progress Across Sessions

```
Coaching without continuity is conversation. Continuity creates development.

Per-person coaching thread:
  Coaching topic: {{What they're working on}}
  Commitment made: {{What they said they would do}}
  Review date: {{When they'll report back}}
  Outcome: {{What happened — met, partially met, not met, and why}}
  
  Always open the next session: "Last time you were going to try {{X}}. What happened?"
  If not done: Explore why without judgment. The barrier is data.
  If done: Celebrate specifically. "What did you learn from that?"

Coaching effectiveness signals:
  Positive: They start the session with "I tried what we discussed and here's what happened"
  Positive: They bring new options they generated themselves
  Positive: They ask for coaching proactively — they've seen it help
  Concern: They do the same topic repeatedly without applying anything
  Concern: They say "yes" to commitments but don't follow through
  Concern: They only do what's coaching-assigned, not self-directed

When coaching isn't working:
  After 3 sessions on the same topic with no progress: explore what's actually in the way
  "We've talked about this three times. What's actually stopping you?"
  Sometimes the answer reveals something the coaching cannot address alone
  → Invoke: skills/people/motivation.md if motivation is the barrier
  → Invoke: skills/people/burnout.md if capacity is the barrier
```

### Step 5 — The Coaching vs. Therapy Boundary

```
Coaching stays within:
  ✓ Work goals, skill development, professional behavior
  ✓ Challenges within the work context
  ✓ Self-awareness as it relates to professional effectiveness
  ✓ Relationship dynamics in the work context

Coaching does not enter:
  ✗ Personal life, trauma, mental health history
  ✗ Medical or clinical concerns
  ✗ Family relationships or childhood experiences
  ✗ Anything that feels like psychological excavation

When conversations approach the boundary:
  "I want to make sure I'm giving you what you need here. What you're describing sounds
  like it might be beyond what a work coaching conversation can fully address. Have you
  considered speaking with someone who specializes in this area?"
  
  Then: Return to the work-adjacent dimensions of the topic
  Never abandon someone mid-conversation — transition carefully
```

## Decision Tree

```
What does the coaching assessment reveal?

├── PERSON NEEDS DIRECTION, NOT COACHING (MODE A)
│   └── Coaching is the wrong tool here — it will frustrate them
│       Switch to teaching mode: explain, demonstrate, then observe
│       "Let me show you how I'd approach this, and then I want to hear your questions."
│       Return to coaching once they have a foundation to build from

├── COMMITMENT SCORE BELOW 7
│   └── Wrong option was chosen, or the goal is not authentically theirs
│       Explore: "What makes you less than 7 on this?"
│       If goal mismatch: Return to Goal phase — reopen what they actually want to work on
│       If option mismatch: Return to Options phase — explore what feels more right

├── SAME TOPIC FOR 3+ SESSIONS WITHOUT MOVEMENT
│   └── Something is in the way that the coaching isn't reaching
│       Name it directly: "We've been here a few times. What's actually in the way?"
│       The barrier may be motivation, capacity, anxiety, or a structural blocker
│       → Invoke: skills/people/motivation.md or skills/people/burnout.md

├── PERSON DEFERRING ALL DECISIONS TO EM
│   └── Coaching dependency — they want direction, not coaching
│       May be Mode A (needs direction) or over-managed (learned to seek permission)
│       If over-managed: Explicitly return ownership: "I'm not going to answer that.
│       What do YOU think the right answer is?"
│       Build toward Mode D by increasing trust and reducing check-ins

├── COACHING CONVERSATION APPROACHING CLINICAL TERRITORY
│   └── "I notice we're getting into some territory that a work conversation can't fully hold.
│       I want to make sure you have the right support for this."
│       Offer to connect to EAP or other resources
│       Transition back to work-related dimensions with care

└── COACHING WORKING — PERSON GENERATING OPTIONS AND FOLLOWING THROUGH
    └── Reinforce the pattern: "Notice that you came up with that yourself."
        Increase challenge: offer harder coaching topics
        Build toward self-coaching: "What question would you ask yourself here?"
```

## Confidence Score

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Coaching readiness assessed before applying GROW | Base: 65% |
| All four GROW phases completed | +15% |
| Commitment captured with specificity (action + date) | +10% |
| Prior commitments reviewed at session start | +10% |
| Person generated options before EM offered any | +5% |
| EM applied coaching when direction was needed | −25% |
| Will phase skipped (no commitment produced) | −20% |
| EM provided the answer they already had in mind | −30% (disqualifying) |

## Failure Modes

| Mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading the witness** | EM asks questions that all point to the answer they wanted anyway | If you already know what you want them to do, give direction. Coaching is for genuine uncertainty. |
| **Skipping Will** | Great options generated; no specific commitment made | Will phase is where development happens. Insight without commitment is interesting conversation. |
| **Mode mismatch** | Coaching a new engineer who doesn't know what to try | Assess readiness first. Direction builds the foundation; coaching builds the insight. |
| **Coaching without continuity** | Every session starts fresh; no follow-up on prior commitments | Open every coaching session: "What happened with what you committed to?" |
| **Rescuing from silence** | Person is thinking; EM fills the silence with suggestions | Count to 10 before speaking. The answer after a long pause is usually the real insight. |
